If your goal is a well-known university abroad, the programme a student follows in the last school years matters a great deal. The British education system, the most widely recognised in the world, splits this journey into two stages: IGCSE and A-Level.

## What is IGCSE?

IGCSE (International General Certificate of Secondary Education) is the first stage of international secondary education, designed for students aged 14–16. Students choose several subjects based on their interests and strengths, and build a solid foundation for the next stage.

IGCSE is a reliable way to prepare for the more demanding A-Level stage, both academically and mentally.

## What is A-Level?

A-Level (Advanced Level) is an in-depth pre-university programme for students aged 16–18. It usually covers the last two years of school.

Students choose 3 or 4 main subjects that match the degree they want to study, and learn them in depth.

## Why A-Level?

- **No foundation year:** with only a local school certificate, British and European universities often require an extra one-year foundation programme. A-Levels let you apply straight to the first year of a bachelor’s degree, without that extra year and its cost.
- **Recognised worldwide:** leading universities in the UK (including Oxford, Cambridge and the Russell Group), the USA, Canada, Germany and Italy accept A-Levels as a reliable entry qualification. Exact requirements depend on the university and the course.

## Which stage should you start with?

- **Students in grades 8–9** usually fit the IGCSE and A-Level route: first the foundation, then A-Level.
- **Students in grades 10–11** and school leavers usually go straight to A-Level.
